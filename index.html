A map of how I got here

Yiguo Zhang

I spent years certain I had discovered new laws of physics. I invented a religion, designed a perpetual motion machine, and named an effect after myself. Then I learned enough to prove myself wrong, over and over, until I started getting things right.

This is the road from the dreamer to the tester.

The Bamboo Coast · ages 0 to 11

A performer who needed a crowd

I was born in China and spent my first years in a village two thousand meters up, in a bamboo forest, growing up half wild. My father's work moved us to a new city every year or two, so nothing stayed. Friends did not last. Toys got left behind.

What lasted was the stage. My father pushed me into singing at seven, and I was terrified of it before I loved it. Six years later I was competing across the province and performing for crowds. I was a performer, and I was good at it. I needed the room.

  • Singing

    Six years of competitive vocal performance in China, with repeated top-three finishes at provincial competitions.

  • Stage

    Performed at community and county events, including original songs written for the occasion.

  • First builds

    Built cardboard guitars at home and performed full sets to an audience of two: my parents.

The Crossing · 2020 to 2021

The audience disappeared

A cancelled flight on a vacation turned into staying in America for good. I arrived at eleven knowing almost no English: yes, no, and the alphabet song. Covid closed the schools a month later. The crowd I had built my whole self around was simply gone.

I became my family's way through everything official: taxes, social security, the green card, interpreting at appointments, the phone trees I could not parse. School was noise I could not decode. So I built a way across the gap myself. Five new words a day, never fewer. Games where I had to talk fast or lose. One narrow bridge, and I kept walking it.

  • At eleven

    Landed in a US classroom mid-semester with effectively no English, relying on translators for the first year.

  • Family interface

    Became the household's translator and document manager: taxes, SSN, the green card, and interpreting at official appointments.

  • Self-taught English

    A daily vocabulary discipline plus full immersion through games, until the language stopped being a wall.

Valley of Invented Sciences · 7th to 8th grade

I believed all of it

Alone after school with the whole internet, I went looking for wonder and found pseudoscience. Aliens, lost civilizations, free energy, the law of attraction. I believed every word, and I treated it like research. I took notes. I built tier lists of souls. I tried to craft items from a video game in real life.

None of it was real. But underneath the nonsense I was doing something that was: chasing an idea to its end, writing everything down, and finally testing it. I built the perpetual motion machine, watched it fail, worked out exactly why, and named the file this doesn't work. That was the first real experiment of my life.

  • A 12-page cosmology

    An entire invented religion explaining the origin of the universe, stitched together from videos I was sure were true.

  • "this doesn't work"

    A perpetual motion machine, a written report claiming it would revolutionize energy, a build, a failure, and a correct diagnosis of why.

  • A named law

    The "ferromagnetic amplification effect," which I was thrilled to discover already had a name and a century of prior work.

  • Antigravity clue files

    Numbered research-journal entries hunting for hidden propulsion tech, ending, tellingly, in real government patents.

  • A Collatz spreadsheet

    Every trajectory from 1 to 100 encoded as a string, hunting five months for a pattern in an unsolved conjecture.

  • One stray line

    Buried in a notebook of nonsense: "I don't like the idea of dark matter." Years later that instinct became a real paper.

The Forge · the constant

The same hands, the whole way down

Step back and the through-line is obvious. The kid building cardboard guitars, the singer, the inventor of fake sciences, the engineer: one urge, different materials. Only the surface ever changed.

At school I pointed it at real things. I fused brass and copper into a Mokume-gane ring over a week of after-school sessions. I co-led the climber on our robotics team and cut a critical part's stress nearly in half in simulation. I noticed broken things in my dorm and fixed them for everyone. I built rubber-band plane kits and a physics manual to put in the hands of fifth graders.

  • Visual Arts Award 2024

    School-wide Visual Arts Department Award, anchored by the forged brass-and-copper ring.

  • Robotics (FRC)

    Co-lead on the climber mechanism; reduced a critical component's simulated stress from roughly 1500 to 800 PSI.

  • Dorm builds

    Unprompted, community-scale fixes: drain covers, shower platforms, and automatic window openers across the dorm.

  • STEM Stewards kits

    Lead engineer on rubber-band plane kits, including a build manual and video, aimed at sparking physics for younger kids.

The Highlands · the payoff

The appetite met the discipline

The dreamer's hunger finally met a tester's method. I taught myself a twelve-hundred-page college physics textbook, page by page, filling a notebook with hundreds of pages of notes. I self-studied one of the hardest AP physics courses in nine days and scored a four. Then I started doing the real thing.

Physics is the field I am headed into, and quantum mechanics is its spine. I built a quantum model of a classic game theory problem and found new solutions that beat the rational one. I tested a climate hypothesis against two decades of satellite data and found it did not hold. And that stray middle-school line about dark matter grew up into a real paper.

  • Published · quantum game theory

    Centipede's Leap Into the Quantum Realm. A quantum treatment of the centipede game yielding new equilibria superior to the classical solution, confirmed in Qiskit. On arXiv and accepted to a journal.

    Read the paper →
  • In submission · dark matter

    Portable Correction Framework for ULDM Soliton Cores Deformed by Black Holes and Baryonic Potentials. Original work on how ultralight dark matter soliton cores deform under realistic galactic conditions. Currently under conference submission.

  • Completed · climate

    The Iris Illusion in the Tropical Sky. Two decades of satellite ice-water and sea-surface data, yielding a Pearson correlation of −0.49 that runs against the iris hypothesis.

  • Foundation

    Self-taught a 1200-page college physics text with hundreds of pages of handwritten notes; self-studied AP Physics C: E&M in nine days for a score of 4.

Where it leads

A fish that stopped measuring itself by flight

For a long time I judged myself against someone I was never going to be. The fix was not to become him. It was to stop weighing a fish by how well it flies. I am headed into quantum physics, still chasing the same feeling I got from the first idea that ever clicked. The difference now is that I check whether it is true.

The dreamer did not die. He learned the rules.
